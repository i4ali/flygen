Today — the wizard

Intent› Type› Text fields› Style› Mood› Colors› Format› QR & Logo› Extras› Review› Generate
~3–4 minutes of taps · after a 12-screen onboarding · 10 credits per generation, 10 per refine, 10 per resize — every experiment costs money.

Concept — the conversation

"Flyer for our church bake sale this Saturday 10am–2pm at Grace Community Hall — proceeds go to the youth summer camp!"
~60–90 seconds to a finished flyer · AI asks one follow-up, shows 3 directions · iterate, resize and retry freely under Pro.
Why the two changes belong together A chat interface invites iteration — "warmer", "bigger headline", "now as a story" — and credits punish exactly that behavior at 10 per try. Meanwhile a subscription needs a reason to recur — and conversations create one: threads you resume, brands the app remembers, bulletins and newsletters that draft themselves every week. Scrapping the wizard without scrapping credits (or vice versa) forfeits the synergy; together they turn a tool you operate into a service you keep.

± The honest ledger

✦ What's gained

  • Time-to-flyer: ~3–4 min of forms → ~90 s of conversation
  • Fearless iteration — refining no longer costs 10 credits a try
  • New surface area: newsletters, recurring series, scan-&-remake
  • Compounding retention: threads to resume, a brand the app remembers, seasonal nudges
  • Simpler app: 4 tabs and a 12-screen onboarding collapse into a list and a chat
  • Languages get easier: type Urdu, get Urdu — no pickers

⚠ What's risked

  • Full rebuild — nearly every screen is replaced; months, not weeks
  • Unit economics of "unlimited" — needs fair-use caps + cheap draft renders (the one number to model first)
  • New dependency: an LLM conversation layer on top of today's image-only pipeline (cost + latency + failure modes)
  • Blank-page anxiety for non-typers — chips must carry them (Journey 7C)
  • Power users lose explicit knobs — canvas tools must absorb that
  • Migration: credit buyers and wizard muscle memory need a graceful path

Verdict — is it worth pursuing? Yes, conditionally. The concept multiplies what already works (fast generation, brand kit, 5 languages, edit-capable pipeline) and deletes the two things taxing it (form friction and credit anxiety). The bet hinges on three things: extraction quality (does one sentence reliably become correct fields?), subscription economics (is a heavy user profitable at ~$4–5/mo?), and rebuild appetite. Cheapest validation: ship Model A — a single chat thread wired to the existing Cloudflare→Gemini pipeline — to TestFlight users before committing to the canvas, series, or newsletter layers. The thread alone answers "do people prefer talking to the wizard?" for a fraction of the rebuild.
Built from a study of the current FlyGen app: 15 categories, the 9-step creation wizard, 12-screen onboarding, 48 templates, brand kit, 5-language prompt system, credit costs (10/generation · 10/refine · 10/resize · 15 free), CloudKit-only identity, and the Cloudflare→OpenRouter→Gemini-3-Pro image pipeline.
