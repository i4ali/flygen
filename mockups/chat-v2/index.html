The whole pitch in one comparison

Today — the wizard + credits

Intent› Type› Text fields› Style› Mood› Colours› Format› QR & logo› Review› Generate
~3–4 minutes of taps, after a 12-screen onboarding. 10 credits per generation, 10 per refine, 10 per resize — every experiment costs money.

Concept — the conversation

"Flyer for our church bake sale this Saturday 10–2 at Grace Hall — proceeds go to the youth summer camp."

~60–90 seconds to a finished flyer. The assistant fills in what it can, asks only what it can't infer, plans the design like a pro before generating, suggests a format and colours, and shows three directions. Iterate, resize and retry freely under one subscription.
Why the two changes belong together

A chat interface invites iteration — "warmer", "bigger headline", "now make it a newsletter" — and credits punish exactly that at 10 a try. A subscription, meanwhile, needs a reason to recur — and conversations create one: threads you resume, a brand the app remembers, bulletins that redraft themselves every month. Scrapping the wizard without scrapping credits forfeits the synergy. Together they turn a tool you operate into a service you keep.

How it behaves like a design expert

The difference between a chatbot and a designer is that it has opinions and shows its work. These seven behaviours are woven through both flows — look for the labelled moments.

🎓

Knows what great looks like

Carries a working knowledge of what each format needs — a fundraiser flyer vs. a sale, a lead story vs. a footer — and runs that checklist out loud before generating, saying what it's optimising for. A trained eye, not a prompt box.

🎯

Asks only what's needed

Reads your brief and asks just what it can't infer — nothing if it's already complete, a few smart follow-ups if it's thin. The number of questions flexes to the task; it's never a fixed form.

📐

Recommends a format

Asks where it's going — Instagram, print, a noticeboard — then picks the aspect ratio for you and says why, instead of making you choose blind.

🎨

Proposes colour directions

Offers two or three named palettes as tappable swatches ("Warm & homemade", "Bold & modern") rather than a colour wheel.

🖼️

Shows past work & samples

Can pull up flyers you've made before and curated examples inline — "want it like last year's, or something fresh?"

◎

Remembers your brand

Logo, colours and tone are recalled automatically, so the second design already looks like it belongs to you.

🔁

Offers to recur

"Make this a monthly series" — the assistant pre-drafts next month and nudges you, turning one design into a habit.

The honest ledger

✦ What's gained

  • Time-to-design: ~3–4 min of forms → ~90 s of conversation
  • Fearless iteration — refining no longer costs credits a try
  • New surface area: newsletters, recurring series, scan-and-remake
  • Compounding retention: threads to resume, a brand remembered, monthly nudges
  • Simpler app: four tabs and a 12-screen onboarding collapse into a list and a chat

⚠ What's risked

  • Full rebuild — nearly every screen is replaced; months, not weeks
  • Economics of "unlimited" — needs fair-use caps + cheap draft renders (model this first)
  • New dependency: an LLM conversation layer over today's image-only pipeline
  • Blank-page anxiety for non-typers — the chips must carry them
  • Migration: credit buyers and wizard muscle memory need a graceful path

? Open questions

  • Real weekly + monthly numbers and trial length
  • Free-tier size — how many designs before the wall?
  • Is the newsletter Pro-gated, or is one free issue the bait?
  • Which model drives the conversation, and what does a turn cost?
  • Where do threads live — on-device + CloudKit, or a real backend?
Verdict — worth pursuing?

Yes, conditionally. The concept multiplies what already works (fast generation, brand kit, an edit-capable pipeline) and deletes the two things taxing it (form friction and credit anxiety). The bet hinges on three things: extraction quality (does one sentence reliably become correct fields?), subscription economics (is a heavy user profitable?), and rebuild appetite. Cheapest validation: ship the pure-chat flyer thread on the existing pipeline to TestFlight before building the canvas, newsletter and series layers.

A fresh, focused take scoped to two content types — start with the flyer flow, then the newsletter flow, then pricing.
